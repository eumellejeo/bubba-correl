## PART 1: EASY QUESTIONS (Questions 1 to 20)

### 1. Under the Architecture Licensure Examination (LEA) syllabus and Diversified Architectural Experience (DAE) guidelines, which sub-category comprises the largest mandatory credit requirement for apprenticeship, and what is its minimum threshold?

A. Contract Documentation & Specifications; 1,000 credit hours (25%)  
B. Architectural Designing, Drafting, & Structural Conceptualization; 1,152 credit hours (30%)  
C. Construction Supervision & Site Coordination; 960 credit hours (20%)  
D. Project Costing, Feasibility, & Estimates; 768 credit hours (15%)

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** The Licensure Examination for Architects (LEA) guidelines and the 2-year logbook for Diversified Architectural Experience designate Field of Practice Sub-Category A (Architectural Designing, Drafting, and Structural Conceptualization) as 30% of the candidate's total apprenticeship, requiring a minimum of 1,152 credit hours. Candidates often mistakenly prioritize construction management or contract documentation as their primary hours, but the board strictly mandates architectural design and structural conceptualization as the dominant professional foundation.

### 2. How does the architectural licensure syllabus define "Structural Conceptualization"?

A. The mathematical calculation of bending moments, shear forces, and steel rebar area requirements for structural elements  
B. The act of conceiving, choosing, and developing the type, disposition, arrangement, and proportioning of structural elements with due regard to safety, economy, functionality, and aesthetics  
C. The direct application of computer-aided structural engineering finite element software to analyze building frames  
D. The supervision and material verification of concrete pouring, formwork stripping, and steel reinforcement installation on site

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** Structural conceptualization is defined specifically in professional practice and licensure syllabi as the architect's act of conceiving, choosing, disposing, arranging, and proportioning structural members to achieve safety, cost-effectiveness, utility, and aesthetics. Distractors A and C describe structural engineering analysis and numerical computation, which are outside the architect's legal scope of practice.

### 3. In engineering mechanics, what distinguishes a scalar quantity from a vector quantity?

A. A scalar has direction only, whereas a vector possesses both magnitude and mass  
B. A scalar possesses magnitude only, whereas a vector is characterized by both magnitude and a defined direction along a line of action  
C. A scalar applies exclusively to dynamic bodies, whereas a vector applies exclusively to static rigid bodies  
D. A scalar is measured exclusively in Newtons, whereas a vector is measured in kilograms

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** Physical quantities in engineering mechanics are divided into scalars, which are completely described by a single real number representing magnitude with appropriate units (e.g., mass, density, length, volume), and vectors, which require both magnitude and directional orientation. Board examinees frequently confuse scalar quantities like mass (\(m\)) with vector quantities like force (\(F\)) or gravitational weight (\(W\)).

### 4. Which of the following load types acts over an infinitesimally small contact area and is idealized as acting through a single point on a structural free-body diagram?

A. Linear distributed load  
B. Surface distributed load  
C. Concentrated (point) load  
D. Body force

- **Correct Answer:** C
- **Technical Rationale:** A concentrated or point force acts across a very narrow contact area treated mathematically as a single point, with units expressed in kilonewtons (\(\text{kN}\)), typical of column reactions, tension hanger rods, and truss node connections. Linear forces act along a line (\(\text{kN/m}\)), surface forces act across an area (\(\text{kN/m}^2\)), and body forces act throughout a material's volume.

### 5. What physical quantity is classified as a "body force" because it acts on every particle throughout the volume of a structural member without physical surface contact?

A. Hydrostatic water pressure against a basement wall  
B. Wind suction pressure across an exterior cladding panel  
C. Gravitational force acting upon the material's mass density  
D. Friction developed along the perimeter of an embedded pile

- **Correct Answer:** C
- **Technical Rationale:** Body forces are generated by force fields (predominantly Earth's gravity in architectural engineering) that act through the volume of a body without requiring physical contact with another object, establishing self-weight as \(\text{density} \times \text{volume}\). Distractors A, B, and D are all surface contact forces acting along external interfaces.

### 6. By applying Newton's Second Law of Motion (\(F = ma\)), what exact physical definition corresponds to one Newton (\(1\text{ N}\)) in the SI metric system?

A. The force required to accelerate a mass of \(1\text{ kg}\) at a rate of \(1\text{ m/s}^2\)  
B. The gravitational mass of \(1\text{ kg}\) at standard sea level atmospheric pressure  
C. The force exerted by a \(9.81\text{ kg}\) mass resting statically upon a horizontal surface  
D. The work performed when a force of \(1\text{ kg}\) acts over a displacement of \(1\text{ meter}\)

- **Correct Answer:** A
- **Technical Rationale:** One Newton (\(\text{N}\)) is defined as the force required to impart an acceleration of \(1\text{ m/s}^2\) to a mass of \(1\text{ kg}\) (\(\text{N} = \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}^2\)). A common board exam pitfall is confusing mass (\(1\text{ kg}\)) with gravitational force (\(1\text{ kg} \times 9.81\text{ m/s}^2 = 9.81\text{ N}\)).

### 7. For a two-dimensional coplanar force system acting on a rigid body to maintain static equilibrium, which set of mathematical conditions must be simultaneously satisfied?

A. \(\sum F_x = 0\) and \(\sum F_y = 0\), while \(\sum M\) may have a net positive value  
B. \(\sum F_x = 0\), \(\sum F_y = 0\), and \(\sum M = 0\)  
C. \(\sum F_x + \sum F_y = \sum M\)  
D. \(\sum F_x = \text{maximum}\), \(\sum F_y = \text{minimum}\), and \(\sum M = 0\)

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** Complete static equilibrium in a 2D coplanar system requires that the net translation along both the X-axis and Y-axis vanishes (\(\sum F_x = 0\), \(\sum F_y = 0\)) and the net rotational tendency vanishes (\(\sum M = 0\)). If only the forces sum to zero but a moment exists, the body will undergo pure rotation (couple action).

### 8. What fundamental structural law governs the straight-line portion of the stress-strain diagram from the origin to the proportional limit, establishing that stress is directly proportional to strain?

A. Pascal's Principle  
B. Hooke's Law  
C. Poisson's Ratio  
D. Euler's Buckling Theory

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** Formulated by Robert Hooke in 1678, Hooke's Law dictates that within the proportional limit, stress is directly proportional to strain (\(\sigma = E\epsilon\)), where the slope of this linear relationship represents the Modulus of Elasticity (\(E\), Young's Modulus). Distractor C represents the transverse-to-longitudinal strain ratio, while D describes column stability.

### 9. On a standard stress-strain diagram, what is the significance of the "ultimate strength"?

A. The point where the material begins to deform elastically without any applied load  
B. The point of initial yield where plastic deformation begins without an increase in force  
C. The maximum ordinate on the curve, representing the maximum stress the material can endure  
D. The exact point where physical separation and fracture occur

- **Correct Answer:** C
- **Technical Rationale:** The ultimate tensile strength is the highest coordinate (maximum ordinate) on the stress-strain curve, denoting the maximum stress capacity developed before necking and eventual fracture. Distractor D represents the rupture or breaking strength, which occurs at a lower nominal stress level after localized necking.

### 10. How is material "ductility" correctly distinguished from "malleability"?

A. Ductility is the capacity to deform under compressive stress, while malleability is the capacity to deform under tensile stress  
B. Ductility is the capacity to deform under tensile stress without fracturing, while malleability is the capacity to deform under compressive stress  
C. Ductility refers exclusively to brittle failure, while malleability refers to fatigue resistance  
D. Ductility measures surface scratch hardness, while malleability measures thermal expansion

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** Ductility is the mechanical property enabling a material to sustain significant plastic deformation under tensile stress (e.g., drawing into wires or bending reinforcing bars), whereas malleability is the ability to deform plastically under compressive stress (e.g., hammering or rolling into thin sheets). Inverting these two definitions is one of the oldest traps in licensure examinations.

### 11. What mechanical property represents the work done on a unit volume of material as it is stressed gradually from the origin up to its elastic limit, measuring its capacity to absorb energy without permanent deformation?

A. Modulus of Rigidity  
B. Modulus of Resilience  
C. Modulus of Toughness  
D. Plastic Section Modulus

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** The Modulus of Resilience represents the area beneath the stress-strain curve from the origin up to the elastic limit (\(E\)), measured in \(\text{N}\cdot\text{m/m}^3\) (or \(\text{J/m}^3\)), indicating the elastic energy a material absorbs without suffering residual plastic distortion. In contrast, the Modulus of Toughness represents the total area up to the rupture point (\(R\)).

### 12. When a structural steel bar is subjected to uniaxial tensile elongation, its cross-section undergoes lateral thinning. Which elastic constant governs the ratio between this lateral strain and axial strain?

A. Young's Modulus  
B. Modulus of Rupture  
C. Poisson's Ratio  
D. Shear Modulus

- **Correct Answer:** C
- **Technical Rationale:** Poisson's ratio (\(\nu\)) defines the negative ratio of transverse (lateral) strain to longitudinal (axial) strain for a material under uniaxial stress, explaining why tension induces lateral narrowing and compression induces lateral bulging. Examinees should note that speech-to-text audio transcriptions often garble this term into phrases like "yourance ratio."

### 13. Which material behavior is characterized by gradual, permanent, time-dependent deformation occurring under continuous, sustained application of service loads?

A. Fatigue  
B. Stress Relaxation  
C. Creep  
D. Strain Hardening

- **Correct Answer:** C
- **Technical Rationale:** Creep is the long-term, progressive deformation of a material subjected to continuous, sustained mechanical stress over time, highly prevalent in concrete slabs and pre-stressed elements. Fatigue (A) is failure caused by cyclic or repeated stress, while stress relaxation (B) is the time-dependent relief of internal stress under a constant constrained strain.

### 14. In beam mechanics, what curvature profile and physical behavior correspond to a "positive bending moment"?

A. Concave downward curvature producing top-fiber tension and bottom-fiber compression (hogging)  
B. Concave upward curvature producing top-fiber compression and bottom-fiber tension (sagging)  
C. Pure torsional twisting along the longitudinal centroidal axis  
D. Zero curvature where transverse shear stress reaches its absolute maximum

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** A positive bending moment induces a concave-upward curvature (sagging, visually memorized as a "smiling" profile), which crushes the upper fibers in compression and stretches the lower fibers in tension under standard downward gravity loading. Negative moment produces a concave-downward curvature (hogging, or "frowning" profile).

### 15. What structural condition occurs at a beam's "inflection point"?

A. Transverse shear force reaches its maximum possible value  
B. Bending moment is zero, and the beam curvature transitions between concave and convex  
C. Compressive stress reaches the ultimate crushing limit of concrete  
D. Vertical deflection reaches its maximum midspan value

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** An inflection point (or point of contraflexure) occurs where the internal bending moment curve crosses the zero axis (\(M = 0\)), marking the transition of the beam's elastic curve from sagging (positive moment) to hogging (negative moment). It acts theoretically as an internal hinge where flexural stress vanishes.

### 16. What support reaction capability characterizes an idealized "roller support" in beam analysis?

A. It provides resistance against vertical displacement (\(F_y\)), horizontal displacement (\(F_x\)), and rotation (\(M\))  
B. It provides a single reaction force perpendicular to the supporting plane (\(F_y\)), allowing free horizontal translation and rotation  
C. It provides horizontal resistance (\(F_x\)) and moment resistance (\(M\)), but zero vertical resistance  
D. It prevents all angular rotation while permitting two-dimensional planar translation

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** A roller support provides only a single reaction force acting perpendicular to the rolling surface (typically vertical normal force \(F_y\)), leaving the member completely free to translate horizontally (relieving thermal expansion stresses) and free to rotate. A pinned support provides two reactions (\(F_x, F_y\)), while a fixed support provides three reactions (\(F_x, F_y, M\)).

### 17. What is the structural function of intentionally building a slight convex upward curvature ("camber") into a long-span beam, girder, or truss during fabrication?

A. To increase the ultimate shear capacity of the beam's end connections  
B. To counteract and compensate for anticipated downward deflection caused by dead loads  
C. To prevent the occurrence of thermal expansion joints along the span  
D. To eliminate the need for transverse shear stirrups near column supports

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** Camber is an intentional upward curvature engineered into a flexural member so that upon receiving service dead loads, the member deflects into a flat, level plane rather than sagging visibly below the horizontal datum. Camber compensates geometrically for serviceability deflections but does not alter the member's ultimate moment or shear strength.

### 18. What is the absolute minimum quantity and size of longitudinal reinforcing steel bars required in a standard structural reinforced concrete beam?

A. 2 bars of 12 mm diameter  
B. 3 bars of 16 mm diameter  
C. 4 bars of 16 mm diameter  
D. 4 bars of 20 mm diameter

- **Correct Answer:** C
- **Technical Rationale:** Standard reinforced concrete beam detailing requires a minimum of 4 longitudinal bars of 16 mm diameter (placed at the four corners of the stirrup cage) to ensure cage rigidity, crack distribution, and minimal flexural integrity. Providing fewer than four bars creates an unstable rebar cage incapable of supporting perimeter closed stirrups.

### 19. According to the National Structural Code of the Philippines (NSCP) and standard concrete specifications, what is the mandatory minimum concrete cover for reinforced concrete elements permanently cast against and exposed to the earth?

A. 40 mm  
B. 50 mm  
C. 65 mm  
D. 75 mm

- **Correct Answer:** D
- **Technical Rationale:** NSCP Section 420.6 dictates that for concrete cast permanently against and in contact with the ground (such as footings, pile caps, and ground foundation beams without mudsills), the minimum protective concrete cover is 75 mm. This substantial cover protects reinforcing steel from soil moisture, chemical attack, and groundwater corrosion.

### 20. In reinforced concrete floor slab construction, what is the absolute minimum standard slab thickness recognized for general serviceability and fire integrity?

A. 50 mm  
B. 75 mm  
C. 100 mm  
D. 150 mm

- **Correct Answer:** C
- **Technical Rationale:** The standard practical minimum thickness for reinforced concrete floor slabs is 100 mm (approximately 4 inches). While thin topping slabs or precast composite toppings may occasionally measure 75 mm, structural suspended slabs require at least 100 mm to accommodate bottom cover, two layers of orthogonal steel bars, and adequate concrete depth for deflection control.

## PART 2: AVERAGE QUESTIONS (Questions 21 to 40)

### 21. A simple span reinforced concrete beam has a clear span of \(6.0\text{ meters}\) and a rectangular width (\(b\)) of \(200\text{ mm}\). Based on standard NSCP minimum thickness rules for deflection control without computation, what is the minimum required overall beam depth (\(h\))?

A. \(300\text{ mm}\)  
B. \(375\text{ mm}\)  
C. \(400\text{ mm}\)  
D. \(500\text{ mm}\)

- **Correct Answer:** C
- **Technical Rationale:** By NSCP deflection control rules, a simply supported non-prestressed beam requires a minimum depth of \(h = L/16 = 6000\text{ mm} / 16 = 375\text{ mm}\). However, standard architectural-structural proportioning dictates that the minimum beam depth must also satisfy \(h \ge 2b = 2(200\text{ mm}) = 400\text{ mm}\); taking the more stringent governing value yields \(400\text{ mm}\). Examinees who calculate only \(L/16\) will fall into the trap of selecting \(375\text{ mm}\).

### 22. In the design of non-prestressed continuous reinforced concrete beams of span \(L\), how do minimum thickness (\(h\)) requirements compare across support conditions?

A. Simply supported: \(L/16\); One end continuous: \(L/18.5\); Both ends continuous: \(L/21\); Cantilever: \(L/8\)  
B. Simply supported: \(L/21\); One end continuous: \(L/18.5\); Both ends continuous: \(L/16\); Cantilever: \(L/8\)  
C. Simply supported: \(L/16\); One end continuous: \(L/21\); Both ends continuous: \(L/18.5\); Cantilever: \(L/12\)  
D. Simply supported: \(L/8\); One end continuous: \(L/16\); Both ends continuous: \(L/18.5\); Cantilever: \(L/21\)

- **Correct Answer:** A
- **Technical Rationale:** Under NSCP Table 409.3.1.1, continuity introduces negative end moments that reduce midspan deflection, allowing shallower depths: simply supported beams require \(L/16\), one end continuous requires \(L/18.5\), both ends continuous requires \(L/21\), whereas cantilevers, lacking an opposing support, require a substantially deeper section of \(L/8\). Reversing the order between simply supported and continuous spans is a classic board exam trap.

### 23. What is the standard minimum inside bend diameter for a \(20\text{ mm}\) diameter rebar when fabricating main bar hooks or closed stirrups?

A. \(4d_b\) (\(80\text{ mm}\))  
B. \(6d_b\) (\(120\text{ mm}\))  
C. \(8d_b\) (\(160\text{ mm}\))  
D. \(10d_b\) (\(200\text{ mm}\))

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** NSCP rebar fabrication provisions specify that for reinforcing bar diameters from \(10\text{ mm}\) to \(25\text{ mm}\), the minimum inside bend diameter is \(6d_b\) (hence \(6 \times 20\text{ mm} = 120\text{ mm}\)). For bars from \(28\text{ mm}\) to \(32\text{ mm}\), the factor increases to \(8d_b\), and for \(36\text{ mm}\) bars and larger, it increases to \(10d_b\) to prevent micro-fracturing of the steel during cold bending.

### 24. What are the clear spacing limits between parallel main longitudinal reinforcing bars placed in a single horizontal layer inside a concrete beam?

A. Not less than \(1.0d_b\) or \(20\text{ mm}\), whichever is smaller  
B. Not less than \(1.0d_b\) or \(25\text{ mm}\), whichever is larger  
C. Fixed at exactly \(50\text{ mm}\) regardless of bar size  
D. Not less than \(2.0d_b\) or \(40\text{ mm}\), whichever is larger

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** To permit the free flow of coarse aggregates and prevent honeycomb voids around reinforcing cages, the clear horizontal spacing between parallel bars in a layer must not be less than the nominal bar diameter (\(d_b\)) or \(25\text{ mm}\) (\(1\text{ inch}\)), whichever is larger. Examinees often confuse the beam bar spacing limit (\(25\text{ mm}\)) with the column bar spacing limit (\(1.5d_b\) or \(40\text{ mm}\)).

### 25. If concrete pouring for a continuous beam or floor slab is unexpectedly delayed and cannot be completed monolithically, where does structural engineering code permit the installation of a vertical construction joint?

A. Directly over the face of the column support where bending moment is zero  
B. Within the middle third of the beam or slab span where transverse shear force is at its minimum  
C. At exactly the quarter-point (\(L/4\)) where diagonal tension cracks initiate  
D. Directly beneath the heaviest concentrated point load along the girder

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** Construction joints in flexural members should be located within the middle third of the span because transverse shear force (\(V\)) is at or near its minimum, minimizing the danger of joint slippage. Placing a cold joint at the face of a support (Option A) is structurally catastrophic because shear stress reaches its maximum at column faces.

### 26. In the flexural analysis of a reinforced concrete rectangular beam, how is the "effective depth" (\(d\)) precisely defined?

A. The total vertical distance from the top compression fiber to the lowest exterior edge of the beam soffit (\(h\))  
B. The distance from the centroid of the longitudinal tension reinforcement to the extreme compression fiber  
C. The distance between the upper and lower longitudinal reinforcing layers  
D. The total depth of the beam minus one clear concrete cover thickness

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** Effective depth (\(d\)) is strictly defined as the distance measured from the extreme fiber in compression to the geometric centroid of the tensile reinforcing steel area. The concrete lying below this centroid is treated as cracked in flexural tension and contributes nothing to resisting the bending moment, serving only to transfer bond stress and provide protective cover.

### 27. Why are vertical shear stirrups in a simply supported reinforced concrete beam spaced closely near the support columns and spaced wider toward midspan?

A. Flexural bending moments reach their maximum at the supports and vanish at midspan  
B. Transverse shear forces and diagonal tension stresses are highest near the supports and decrease toward the center  
C. Thermal expansion stresses are concentrated at the beam ends due to connection restraint  
D. Column dowels require confinement steel to prevent outward bar buckling

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** In a beam carrying distributed gravity loads, the vertical shear force (\(V\)) varies linearly from a maximum at the supports to zero at midspan, creating steep \(45^\circ\) diagonal tension stresses near the supports that require closely spaced vertical stirrups. Examinees who confuse shear diagrams with moment diagrams often pick Option A.

### 28. For reinforced concrete beams and columns not exposed to weather or earth, what is the standard minimum concrete cover required for primary reinforcement and ties?

A. \(20\text{ mm}\)  
B. \(30\text{ mm}\)  
C. \(40\text{ mm}\)  
D. \(50\text{ mm}\)

- **Correct Answer:** C
- **Technical Rationale:** Under NSCP 420.6.1.3, for reinforced concrete members not exposed to weather or ground contact, columns and beams require a minimum protective cover of \(40\text{ mm}\) to the outer stirrups or ties. A common trap is selecting \(20\text{ mm}\), which is permitted only for interior unexposed slabs and walls.

### 29. A two-way structural floor slab panel measures \(4.0\text{ meters}\) by \(9.0\text{ meters}\) between supporting beam centerlines. Structurally, how will this slab behave under applied gravity loads?

A. It will act as a true two-way slab because it is framed by beams on all four perimeter sides  
B. It will act as a one-way slab transferring virtually all loads to the long supporting beams because its aspect ratio exceeds \(2.0\)  
C. It will behave as a cantilever slab because the long span exceeds \(6.0\text{ meters}\)  
D. It will exhibit flat-plate behavior and fail in two-way punching shear

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** Even if framed by beams on all four sides, a slab with a long-to-short span ratio exceeding \(2.0\) (\(9.0\text{ m} / 4.0\text{ m} = 2.25 > 2.0\)) or short-to-long ratio \(< 0.5\) (\(4/9 = 0.44 < 0.5\)) deflects predominantly in its short, stiff direction, causing over \(90%\) of the load to transfer to the long beams as a one-way slab. Examinees frequently assume that framing on all four sides automatically creates two-way structural action.

### 30. How must reinforcing steel bars be arranged in a cast-in-place one-way floor slab?

A. Main flexural bars are placed parallel to the long span, while temperature bars run parallel to the short span  
B. Main flexural bars are placed parallel to the short span, while temperature and shrinkage bars run parallel to the long span  
C. Both bar layers are sized identically and placed diagonally at \(45^\circ\) across the slab corners  
D. Main bars are positioned exclusively at the top face across the entire midspan zone

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** In a one-way slab, load transfers across the shortest path; therefore, primary flexural reinforcement must run parallel to the short span at the bottom of the slab at midspan to resist positive tension. Temperature and shrinkage reinforcement is placed perpendicular to the main bars (parallel to the long span) to control cracking caused by thermal changes and drying shrinkage.

### 31. According to structural rules of thumb, what is the minimum thickness (\(t\)) for a two-way slab with perimeter beams having a total panel perimeter of \(18.0\text{ meters}\)?

Although some basic codes mention \(90\text{ mm}\) as an absolute mathematical limit for lightly loaded slabs, the practical minimum thickness threshold for cast-in-place structural slabs is rounded up to \(100\text{ mm}\).

### 32. What is the maximum allowable spacing for primary flexural reinforcing bars in a cast-in-place reinforced concrete slab?

- **Technical Rationale:** Under NSCP provisions, the center-to-center spacing of primary flexural reinforcement in slabs must not exceed \(3\) times the slab thickness (\(3t\)) or \(450\text{ mm}\) (\(18\text{ inches}\)), whichever is smaller. Option C (\(5t\) or \(450\text{ mm}\)) governs secondary temperature and shrinkage reinforcement, which is a frequent source of candidate confusion.

### 33. In composite steel deck floor construction, what is the primary structural function of welding headed "shear studs" to the top flange of structural steel beams through the corrugated metal deck?

A. To temporarily secure the metal deck against wind uplift during erection  
B. To ground the steel frame against lightning strikes and electrical short circuits  
C. To transfer horizontal interface shear forces, forcing the concrete slab and steel beam to act compositely as a unified T-beam  
D. To provide vertical leveling shims for screeding the concrete topping

- **Correct Answer:** C
- **Technical Rationale:** Headed shear studs welded through the deck onto the steel beam flange resist horizontal slip along the steel-concrete interface, allowing the concrete slab to function as the compressive flange of a composite T-beam section. While studs do prevent deck movement during pouring, their primary structural purpose is composite action.

### 34. What geometric ratio and primary failure mode differentiate a "short column" from a "long (slender) column"?

A. A column is short if its slenderness ratio (\(L_e / d\)) does not exceed 12 and it fails primarily by material crushing; it is long if \(L_e / d > 12\) and it fails primarily by lateral buckling  
B. A column is short if its height is under \(3.0\text{ meters}\) and it fails by shear rupture; it is long if over \(3.0\text{ meters}\) and it fails by tension pullout  
C. A column is short if its cross-section is square; it is long if its cross-section is circular or rectangular  
D. A column is short if it contains tied reinforcement; it is long if it contains continuous spiral reinforcement

- **Correct Answer:** A
- **Technical Rationale:** Columns are classified mechanically by slenderness: a column is designated as short when its ratio of effective length to least lateral dimension (\(L_e / d\)) is 12 or less, meaning its capacity is governed by concrete and steel compressive crushing. When \(L_e / d > 12\), secondary lateral deflections dominate, and the column becomes susceptible to elastic or inelastic buckling failure.

### 35. For a standard tied rectangular reinforced concrete column, what are the code-mandated minimum column cross-sectional dimension and longitudinal reinforcement requirements?

A. Minimum dimension of \(150\text{ mm}\) and at least 4 bars of \(12\text{ mm}\) diameter  
B. Minimum dimension of \(200\text{ mm}\) and at least 4 bars of \(16\text{ mm}\) diameter

- **Technical Rationale:** NSCP structural detailing mandates that a tied compressive column must have a least lateral dimension of not less than \(200\text{ mm}\) and be reinforced with a minimum of 4 longitudinal bars of \(16\text{ mm}\) diameter. ### 36. What are the mandatory minimum diameter, minimum bar count, and clear spiral pitch limits for a circular spiral column?

### 44. In deep foundation engineering, how does an "end-bearing pile" differ from a "friction pile"?

### 60. In seismic analysis, what defines a structural "Soft Story" irregularity, and how does it differ from a "Weak Story" irregularity?

A. A Soft Story has lateral stiffness $< 70%$ of the story above; a Weak Story has lateral strength $< 80%$ of the story above

- **Correct Answer:** A
