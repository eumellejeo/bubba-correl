## PART 1: EASY QUESTIONS (Questions 1 to 20)

### Question 1

⚠️ [VISUAL REFERENCE REQUIRED: Plumbing detail drawing showing a lavatory faucet terminating above a sink rim, with dimension arrows indicating the vertical distance between the faucet outlet and the fixture flood level rim.]

In potable water supply design, what is the term for the unobstructed vertical distance through the free atmosphere between the lowest opening of any pipe or faucet supplying water to a tank, plumbing fixture, or other device and the flood level rim of the receptacle? A. Trap Seal  
B. Air Gap  
C. Effective Opening  
D. Critical Level

- **Correct Answer:** B. Air Gap
- **Technical Rationale:** An **air gap** is the primary physical defense against backflow and back-siphonage, defined under the plumbing code as the vertical atmospheric separation between a supply outlet and a fixture's flood level rim (mandated to be at least twice the diameter of the supply pipe, and never less than 25 mm). The common board-exam trap is confusing the "air gap" with the "effective opening," which is merely the cross-sectional area of the faucet outlet itself, or with the "critical level," which refers to the submergence marking on a vacuum breaker.

### Question 2

Under the National Plumbing Code of the Philippines (NPCP), what is the allowable minimum and maximum vertical depth for a standard liquid trap seal in plumbing drainage fixtures? A. 25 mm minimum to 51 mm maximum  
B. 51 mm minimum to 102 mm maximum  
C. 76 mm minimum to 152 mm maximum  
D. 102 mm minimum to 204 mm maximum

- **Correct Answer:** B. 51 mm minimum to 102 mm maximum
- **Technical Rationale:** Standard P-traps must maintain a water seal depth between 51 mm (2 inches) and 102 mm (4 inches) measured from the crown weir to the top of the dip to prevent sewer gases from entering living spaces without creating excessive hydraulic resistance. Examinees frequently fall into the trap of selecting Option A by confusing the 51 mm minimum trap seal depth with the 25 mm (1-inch) minimum atmospheric air gap dimension.

### Question 3

In sanitary drainage design, what is the standard volumetric discharge rate equivalent to one (1) Drainage Fixture Unit (DFU)? A. 1.0 gallon per minute (0.063 L/s)  
B. 3.5 gallons per minute (0.22 L/s)  
C. 7.5 gallons per minute (0.47 L/s / 1.0 cu. ft./min)  
D. 15.0 gallons per minute (0.95 L/s)

- **Correct Answer:** C. 7.5 gallons per minute (0.47 L/s / 1.0 cu. ft./min)
- **Technical Rationale:** One **Drainage Fixture Unit (DFU)** is calibrated to the discharge of an ordinary domestic washbasin, mathematically defined as 7.5 gallons per minute (approximately 28.3 L/min or 1 cubic foot per minute). Candidates often guess Option A assuming a unit base of "1.0 gpm for 1 unit," confusing fixture unit equivalence with water supply fixture units (WSFU) or direct flow rates.

### Question 4

Which piping material is chemically rated, manufactured, and code-approved specifically for pressurized hot-water distribution lines within building interiors? A. Schedule 40 Polyvinyl Chloride (PVC)  
B. Chlorinated Polyvinyl Chloride (cPVC)  
C. Acrylonitrile Butadiene Styrene (ABS)  
D. High-Density Polyethylene (HDPE) corrugated pipe

- **Correct Answer:** B. Chlorinated Polyvinyl Chloride (cPVC)
- **Technical Rationale:** Post-chlorinated resin gives **cPVC** the thermal stability required to withstand operating temperatures up to 82°C (180°F) at 100 psi, whereas standard PVC softens and degrades above 60°C (140°F) and is restricted to cold-water or DWV lines. Licensure candidates frequently confuse standard PVC with cPVC or select ABS, which is strictly a rigid drainage, waste, and vent (DWV) material.

D. George Jennings

- **Correct Answer:** B. Alexander Cummings

### Question 6

According to Ohm’s Law, which equation correctly describes the relationship between electromotive force (Voltage, \(V\)), current intensity (Amperes, \(I\)), and circuit resistance (Ohms, \(R\))? A. \(V = I \times R\)  
B. \(I = V \times R\)  
C. \(R = V \times I\)  
D. \(V = I / R\)

- **Correct Answer:** A. \(V = I \times R\)
- **Technical Rationale:** **Ohm’s Law** dictates that electrical potential difference (\(V\)) equals the product of current flow (\(I\)) and the opposing resistance (\(R\)). The common distractor trap is Option B, which inverted algebraic manipulation causes examinees to select under time pressure instead of \(I = V / R\).

### Question 7

What is the fundamental operational difference between Direct Current (DC) and Alternating Current (AC) in electrical distribution? A. DC reverses its polarity 60 times per second, whereas AC remains strictly unidirectional.  
B. DC maintains a constant unidirectional flow of electrons, whereas AC periodically reverses its direction and magnitude in a sinusoidal cycle.  
C. AC cannot be stepped up or down using transformers, whereas DC voltage is easily transformed.  
D. DC is used exclusively for long-distance utility high-voltage transmission grids.

- **Correct Answer:** B. DC maintains a constant unidirectional flow of electrons, whereas AC periodically reverses its direction and magnitude in a sinusoidal cycle.
- **Technical Rationale:** **Direct Current (DC)** flows uniformly in one direction (produced by batteries and photovoltaic cells), whereas **Alternating Current (AC)** oscillates sinusoidally (typically 60 Hz in the Philippines and the US), allowing efficient voltage stepping via electromagnetic transformers. Board candidates often reverse the definitions or mistakenly believe transformers operate on direct current.

### Question 9

In architectural illumination physics, what is the photometric definition of **Luminous Flux**, and what is its standard metric unit of measurement? A. Luminous intensity in a specific angular direction, measured in Candela.  
B. Total quantity of light energy emitted by a light source per second in all directions, measured in Lumens.  
C. Density of light falling across a unit surface area, measured in Lux.

- **Correct Answer:** B. Total quantity of light energy emitted by a light source per second in all directions, measured in Lumens.

Candidates frequently confuse luminous flux with luminous intensity (candela, Option A) or area illuminance (lux, Option C).

### Question 10

When converting lighting calculations between English and SI systems, one footcandle (1 fc) of illuminance is equivalent to approximately how many metric lux? A. 1.0 lux  
B. 3.28 lux  
C. 10.76 lux  
D. 100.0 lux

- **Correct Answer:** C. 10.76 lux
- **Technical Rationale:** Because 1 foot equals 0.3048 meters, 1 square foot equals \(0.0929\text{ m}^2\); therefore, \(1\text{ lumen/ft}^2\) (1 footcandle) equals \(1 / 0.0929 \approx \mathbf{10.76\text{ lux}}\) (lumens per square meter). The major distractor is 3.28, which is the linear conversion from meters to feet rather than the squared area conversion factor.

### Question 11

Which of the following commercial lamp technologies delivers the highest theoretical luminous efficacy (measured in lumens per watt)? A. Standard Incandescent A-19 lamp  
B. Tungsten Halogen lamp  
C. High-Pressure Mercury Vapor lamp  
D. Low-Pressure Sodium (LPS) lamp

- **Correct Answer:** D. Low-Pressure Sodium (LPS) lamp
- **Technical Rationale:** **Low-Pressure Sodium (LPS)** lamps achieve efficacies up to 150–200 lm/W because almost their entire radiation is concentrated in the monochromatic yellow-amber wavelength (589 nm), which is right near the peak sensitivity of the human photopic eye. Examinees often select incandescent or halogen lamps due to their widespread use, or mercury vapor because of its industrial designation, despite its inferior efficacy (30–50 lm/W).

### Question 12

⚠️ [VISUAL REFERENCE REQUIRED: Closed-loop thermodynamic mechanical diagram depicting the four stages of the mechanical refrigeration cycle, showing refrigerant transitions between vapor, liquid, high-pressure, and low-pressure states.]

In a mechanical vapor-compression air conditioning system, what are the four primary components of the closed thermodynamic cycle in sequential order of refrigerant flow? A. Evaporator \(\rightarrow\) Condenser \(\rightarrow\) Compressor \(\rightarrow\) Expansion Valve  
B. Compressor \(\rightarrow\) Condenser \(\rightarrow\) Expansion Valve \(\rightarrow\) Evaporator  
C. Condenser \(\rightarrow\) Evaporator \(\rightarrow\) Expansion Valve \(\rightarrow\) Compressor  
D. Expansion Valve \(\rightarrow\) Compressor \(\rightarrow\) Evaporator \(\rightarrow\) Condenser

- **Correct Answer:** B. Compressor \(\rightarrow\) Condenser \(\rightarrow\) Expansion Valve \(\rightarrow\) Evaporator
- **Technical Rationale:** The refrigerant vapor is pressurized by the **compressor** (superheated high-pressure gas), rejects heat to the environment and liquefies in the **condenser** (high-pressure liquid), passes through the **expansion valve** (dropping pressure and temperature via flashing), and absorbs room heat in the **evaporator** (boiling into a low-pressure gas). Examinees frequently mix up the cycle sequence, particularly swapping the locations of the condenser and evaporator.

### Question 15

What is the fundamental mechanical difference between a conventional hydraulic passenger elevator and a geared electric traction passenger elevator? A. ### Question 16

What are the three essential components that form the foundational **Fire Triangle** required for the ignition and maintenance of combustion? A. Smoke, Carbon Monoxide, and Nitrogen  
B. Fuel, Oxygen, and Heat  
C. Fuel, Carbon Dioxide, and Spark

### Question 18

What is the universally accepted frequency range of human hearing across the acoustic spectrum? A. 2 Hz to 200 Hz  
B. 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz (20 kHz)  
C. 200 Hz to 2,000 Hz  
D. 20,000 Hz to 200,000 Hz

- **Correct Answer:** B. 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz (20 kHz)
- **Technical Rationale:** The human ear can detect acoustic air vibrations ranging from approximately **20 Hz** (very deep bass) to **20,000 Hz** (extremely high treble), with peak sensitivity occurring in the speech intelligibility range between 1,000 Hz and 4,000 Hz. Frequencies below 20 Hz are termed infrasonic (Option A), while those above 20 kHz are ultrasonic (Option D).

### Question 20

How is the **Noise Reduction Coefficient (NRC)** of an architectural building finish material calculated? A. The sound transmission loss measured at a single frequency of 500 Hz.

### Question 21

⚠️ [VISUAL REFERENCE REQUIRED: Isometric plumbing riser diagram of a public commercial restroom battery showing 4 flushometer water closets, 4 lavatories, and 2 wall-hung blowout urinals discharging into a horizontal branch line.]

An architect is reviewing the sizing calculations for a commercial battery restroom containing 4 flushometer-valve water closets (public), 4 lavatories (public), and 2 blowout urinals with flushometers. Using the National Plumbing Code of the Philippines (NPCP) schedule (Water Closet = 6 DFU; Lavatory = 1 DFU; Blowout Urinal = 6 DFU), what is the total cumulative Drainage Fixture Unit (DFU) load on the horizontal collecting branch drain? A. 24 DFU  
B. 32 DFU  
C. 40 DFU  
D. 52 DFU

- **Correct Answer:** C. 40 DFU
- **Technical Rationale:** Multiplying each fixture count by its code DFU: 4 water closets \(\times 6\text{ DFU} = 24\text{ DFU}\); 4 lavatories \(\times 1\text{ DFU} = 4\text{ DFU}\); 2 blowout urinals \(\times 6\text{ DFU} = 12\text{ DFU}\). The total branch load is \(24 + 4 + 12 = \mathbf{40\text{ DFU}}\). The common trap is treating urinals as standard wall-hung washout/siphon-jet units (2 DFU each, totaling 32 DFU), missing the heavy 6 DFU loading designated specifically for blowout urinals.

### Question 22

Under the NPCP, what is the standard mandated slope for horizontal sanitary drainage lines 76 mm (3 inches) or less in diameter, and what specific variance is allowed for larger pipes? A. Standard slope is 1% (10 mm/m); variance allows 0.5% (5 mm/m) for 100 mm pipes.  
B. Standard slope is 2% (20 mm/m or \(1/4''\text{ per foot}\)); lines 102 mm (4 inches) or larger may be sloped at 1% (10 mm/m or \(1/8''\text{ per foot}\)) with special administrative approval.  
C. Standard slope is 4% (40 mm/m or \(1/2''\text{ per foot}\)) for all underground piping.  
D. Standard slope is 0.5% for gravity flow to prevent water separating from solids.

- **Correct Answer:** B. Standard slope is 2% (20 mm/m or \(1/4''\text{ per foot}\)); lines 102 mm (4 inches) or larger may be sloped at 1% (10 mm/m or \(1/8''\text{ per foot}\)) with special administrative approval.
- **Technical Rationale:** The NPCP mandates a **2%** (20 mm per meter) slope for horizontal pipes to maintain a self-scouring velocity of at least 0.6 m/s (2 ft/s); where physical site or structural obstacles prevent this, pipes 102 mm (4-inch) or larger can drop to **1%** if approved by the authority. Candidates often fail to recognize that slopes under 1% cause solids to settle, while excessively steep slopes cause water to run ahead of solids.

### Question 23

⚠️ [VISUAL REFERENCE REQUIRED: Vertical cross-sectional schematic of a multi-story plumbing soil stack showing waste water plunging from the 5th floor, compressing the air column at the base and blowing trap water out of a 1st-floor water closet.]

In a 10-story residential condominium, a sudden discharge of wastewater down the main vertical soil stack forces foul odors and water droplets to erupt out of the ground-floor water closet bowl. Which drainage failure phenomenon has occurred? A. Direct Self-Siphonage  
B. Momentum Siphonage  
C. Backpressure (Compression)  
D. Capillary Attraction

- **Correct Answer:** C. Backpressure (Compression)
- **Technical Rationale:** When falling slugs of wastewater rush down a high-rise stack, they drag an air column that compresses upon hitting the horizontal house drain offset at the base; if relief venting is inadequate, this positive pneumatic pressure blows out the lowest fixture trap seals into the room. Examinees frequently confuse this with "Momentum Siphonage," which creates negative (aspirating) pressure that sucks water out of traps rather than blowing it outward.

### Question 24

In designing an on-site private sewage disposal system, a standard two-compartment concrete septic tank is sized for a daily liquid capacity of 3,600 liters. Under standard sanitary engineering codes, what must be the liquid capacity of the secondary (digestion/settling) compartment, and what is the minimum required vertical airspace above the liquid level? A. Secondary compartment = 1,800 L; Airspace = 25.4 mm (1 inch)  
B. Secondary compartment = 1,200 L; Airspace = 50.8 mm (2 inches)  
C. Secondary compartment = 2,400 L; Airspace = 100 mm (4 inches)  
D. Secondary compartment = 900 L; Airspace = 150 mm (6 inches)

- **Correct Answer:** B. Secondary compartment = 1,200 L; Airspace = 50.8 mm (2 inches)
- **Technical Rationale:** The code stipulates that the first compartment of a septic tank must hold between two-thirds (\(2/3\)) and three-quarters (\(3/4\)) of total capacity, leaving one-third (\(1/3\), or \(3,600 \times 1/3 = \mathbf{1,200\text{ L}}\)) for the second compartment, with a minimum headspace of **50.8 mm (2 inches)** between the liquid surface and the underside of the cover slab for gas circulation. Examinees often mistakenly divide the tank into equal halves (Option A) or reverse the compartment sizes (Option C).

### Question 25

Under the Philippine Electrical Code (PEC), a branch circuit feeding a commercial lighting installation that operates continuously for 3 hours or more must not have its continuous load exceed what percentage of the total branch-circuit overcurrent protection device (breaker) rating? A. 50%  
B. 70%  
C. 80%  
D. 100%

- **Correct Answer:** C. 80%
- **Technical Rationale:** Continuous loads generate sustained thermal resistance inside circuit breakers; therefore, the PEC requires that the continuous load shall not exceed **80%** of the circuit breaker's nominal ampacity rating (or conversely, the breaker must be sized at 125% of the continuous load). Candidates often assume that because a breaker is rated for 20 Amperes, it can safely sustain a full 20A continuous load (Option D), which would cause nuisance thermal tripping.

### Question 26

An architect is detailing a dedicated 120-Volt branch circuit feeding general-purpose convenience receptacles in a commercial retail space. If the circuit is protected by a 20-Ampere circuit breaker, what is the maximum number of standard duplex convenience receptacles allowed on this single circuit if each outlet is rated at the standard PEC minimum of 180 Volt-Amperes (VA)? A. 8 receptacles  
B. 10 receptacles  
C. 13 receptacles  
D. 16 receptacles

- **Correct Answer:** C. 13 receptacles

Dividing this total volt-ampere rating by the code requirement of 180 VA per outlet yields \(2,400 / 180 = 13.33\), which limits the installation to a maximum of **13 duplex receptacles**. Candidates often erroneously calculate based on a 15-Ampere circuit (\(1,800\text{ VA} / 180\text{ VA} = 10\text{ outlets}\), Option B) or assume 8 outlets based on continuous loading rules.

### Question 29

A direct downlight luminaire with a point-source luminous intensity of 1,600 Candelas directed vertically downward is suspended 4.0 meters directly above a drafting workstation. Using the Inverse Square Law (\(E = I / d^2\)), what is the calculated direct horizontal illuminance arriving at the table surface directly below the fixture? A. 100 lux  
B. 200 lux  
C. 400 lux  
D. 6,400 lux

- **Correct Answer:** A. 100 lux
- **Technical Rationale:** The Inverse Square Law states that illuminance is intensity divided by distance squared: \(E = I / d^2 = 1,600 / (4.0)^2 = 1,600 / 16 = \mathbf{100\text{ lux}}\). Examinees often forget to square the distance and simply divide 1,600 by 4, arriving at the distractor 400 lux (Option C).

### Question 30

In the architectural studio from Question 29, the drafting board is tilted at an angle such that the incident light ray strikes the board surface at an angle of 60° relative to the surface normal. According to Lambert’s Cosine Law (\(E\_\theta = E_n \cos\theta\)), what is the adjusted illuminance on the tilted board? A. 25 lux  
B. 50 lux  
C. 86.6 lux  
D. 100 lux

- **Correct Answer:** B. 50 lux
- **Technical Rationale:** **Lambert’s Cosine Law** states that illuminance across an inclined plane decreases proportionally to the cosine of the angle between the light beam and the surface normal: \(E\_\theta = E_n \cos(60^\circ) = 100\text{ lux} \times 0.50 = \mathbf{50\text{ lux}}\). The common trap is using \(\sin(60^\circ) = 0.866\), yielding 86.6 lux (Option C), which confuses the surface plane angle with the surface normal angle.
