### Question 1

Under the Unified Soil Classification System (USCS, ASTM D2487), how is a coarse-grained soil differentiated from a fine-grained soil?

- A. More than 50% by dry weight is retained on the No. 200 sieve (0.075 mm).
- B. More than 50% by dry weight passes through the No. 4 sieve (4.75 mm).
- C. The soil exhibits a Plasticity Index (PI) greater than 7.
- D. More than 50% by dry weight passes through the No. 200 sieve (0.075 mm).

**Correct Answer:** A  
**Technical Rationale:** Soils are classified as coarse-grained if more than 50% of the total dry specimen is retained on the No. 200 (0.075 mm) mesh sieve; if 50% or more passes through, it is fine-grained (silts and clays). Candidates frequently confuse the No. 4 sieve (which separates gravel from sand) with the No. 200 sieve (which separates coarse particles from fines).

### Question 2

In subterranean termite barrier systems for building siteworks, what is the fundamental functional distinction between a termiticide soil barrier and a stainless steel mesh barrier (such as Termi-Mesh)?

- A. Stainless steel mesh chemically repels foraging termites, whereas termiticides act as an impermeable physical screen.
- B. Termiticide establishes a lethal chemical exclusionary zone in the soil, whereas stainless steel mesh provides a permanent non-toxic physical aperture barrier smaller than a termite's head.
- C. Termiticides remain effective for the design life of the building without replenishment, whereas stainless steel mesh degrades under soil acidity.
- D. Mesh barriers can only be applied to perimeter retaining walls, whereas chemical barriers are exclusively applied beneath floor slabs.

**Correct Answer:** B  
**Technical Rationale:** Chemical termiticides create a treated zone that poisons or repels termites as they forage from the ground, requiring scheduled reapplication. In contrast, marine-grade stainless steel physical barriers feature micromesh apertures (0.18 mm to 0.72 mm) that mechanically block termite entry without chemicals. Examinees often confuse the maintenance lifespan, mistakenly assuming chemical barriers last permanently.

### Question 3

According to ASTM C150 (Standard Specification for Portland Cement), which cement type must be specified for concrete structures exposed to severe sulfate soils or groundwater conditions?

- A. Type I
- B. Type II
- C. Type III
- D. Type V

**Correct Answer:** D  
**Technical Rationale:** ASTM C150 Type V is specifically formulated with low tricalcium aluminate (\(C_3A < 5%\)) to resist extreme sulfate attack from alkaline soils and groundwater. Type II provides moderate sulfate resistance, Type I is for general purpose, and Type III is for high early strength. A common board trap is selecting Type III, confusing rapid curing with chemical durability.

### Question 5

What is the fundamental engineering relationship between the water-cement (\(w/c\)) ratio and the mechanical properties of cured structural concrete, as established by Abram's Law?

- A. Higher \(w/c\) ratios accelerate hydration and increase final 28-day compressive strength.
- B. Lower \(w/c\) ratios decrease capillary porosity, thereby increasing compressive strength and durability.
- C. Lower \(w/c\) ratios maximize workability while holding compressive strength constant.
- D. Higher \(w/c\) ratios decrease overall drying shrinkage and minimize surface cracking.

**Correct Answer:** B  
**Technical Rationale:** Lower water-cement ratios leave fewer unreacted water pockets during hydration, which densifies the calcium-silicate-hydrate paste and significantly raises compressive strength and impermeability. The common board trap is equating high water content with superior consolidation, ignoring the severe capillary voids and drying shrinkage caused by excess water.

### Question 10

Which of the following mortar joint profiles provides the greatest resistance to rainwater infiltration and freeze-thaw spalling in exterior exposed masonry facades?

- A. Raked joint
- B. Struck joint
- C. Concave joint
- D. Flush joint

**Correct Answer:** C

In structural steel designation nomenclature, what do the numbers in the shape designation **W12 x 50** denote?

### Question 12

Which plain carbon steel classification features a carbon content ranging between 0.15% and 0.25%, making it the most versatile and weldable structural steel alloy (e.g., ASTM A36)?

### Question 14

When detailing exterior metallic building envelopes, which pairing of metals presents the highest risk of galvanic corrosion if directly joined in the presence of an electrolyte?

- A. Zinc and galvanized steel
- B. Aluminum cladding directly fastened with uninsulated copper or brass screws

What is the minimum specified yield strength of standard reinforcing steel bars designated as **Grade 40** and **Grade 60** under ASTM A615 / PNS 49?

### Question 16

Botanically and anatomically, what is the primary structural differentiator between softwood and hardwood lumber?

- A. Softwoods are always physically softer and less dense than hardwoods.
- B. Softwoods originate from gymnosperm coniferous trees (evergreens with needles and cones), whereas hardwoods originate from angiosperm deciduous broad-leaved trees.
- C. Softwoods contain vessel elements (pores) for sap transport, whereas hardwoods consist exclusively of tracheid cells.
- D. Softwoods are exclusively used for interior decorative finish carpentry, while hardwoods are used for heavy structural timber framing.

**Correct Answer:** B  
**Technical Rationale:** The division between softwoods and hardwoods is botanical: softwoods are gymnosperms (conifers such as pine, fir, spruce) featuring primitive tracheid cells, while hardwoods are angiosperms (broad-leaved trees such as oak, mahogany, narra) featuring complex vessel pores. Hardness is not the determinant—balsa is a botanical hardwood, while Douglas fir is a softwood with high structural strength.

### Question 18

What is the Fiber Saturation Point (FSP) in wood technology, and at what approximate moisture content (MC) does it typically occur?

- A. The state where all water has been evaporated; occurs at \(0%\text{ MC}\).
- B. The condition where cell cavities are completely full of free water but cell walls are dry; occurs at \(50%\text{ MC}\).
- C. The condition where cell cavities are completely emptied of free water while cell walls remain fully saturated with bound water; occurs at approximately \(28%\text{ to }30%\text{ MC}\).
- D. The equilibrium moisture content of timber under standard ambient room conditions; occurs at \(12%\text{ to }15%\text{ MC}\).

**Correct Answer:** C  
**Technical Rationale:** FSP occurs at roughly \(28%\text{ to }30%\) moisture content. It marks the critical boundary below which the wood begins to lose bound water from its cell walls, triggering volumetric shrinkage and increasing its structural strength. Board candidates often confuse FSP with Equilibrium Moisture Content (EMC).

### Question 19

Which lumber sawing technique produces boards whose annual growth rings meet the broad face at an angle of \(60^\circ\text{ to }90^\circ\), offering maximum dimensional stability and minimal surface cupping?

- A. Plainsawing (flatsawing)
- B. Quartersawing

Plainsawing remains common due to higher timber yield, but it sacrifices stability.

### Question 20

In polymer materials science, what is the fundamental behavioral difference between a thermoplastic polymer (e.g., PVC, acrylic) and a thermosetting polymer (e.g., epoxy, polyurethane)?

- A. Thermoplastics can be repeatedly softened by heating and hardened by cooling, whereas thermosets undergo irreversible chemical cross-linking and decompose rather than melt upon reheating.
- B. Thermosets melt at low temperatures and can be re-extruded, while thermoplastics are permanently rigid.
- C. Thermoplastics contain three-dimensional cross-linked covalent bonds, while thermosets consist solely of linear polymer chains.
- D. Thermosets are used exclusively as flexible vapor barrier membranes, while thermoplastics are used only as rigid adhesives.

**Correct Answer:** A  
**Technical Rationale:** Thermoplastics consist of linear or branched polymer chains held by weak secondary bonds that relax when heated, allowing them to be reshaped repeatedly. ### Question 21

A laboratory soil sample yields a Liquid Limit (LL) of 48% and a Plastic Limit (PL) of 22%. What is the Plasticity Index (PI) of this soil, and how should an architect/engineer evaluate its foundation settlement risk?

- A. \(\text{PI} = 70%\); the soil is completely cohesionless and drains instantly.
- B. \(\text{PI} = 26%\); the soil is moderately-to-highly plastic clay exhibiting high potential for shrink-swell and foundation movement.
- C. \(\text{PI} = 2.18\); the soil is non-expansive clean gravel.
- D. \(\text{PI} = 15%\); the soil is classified as a low-compressibility silt with no expansive hazards.

**Correct Answer:** B  
**Technical Rationale:** \(\text{Plasticity Index (PI)} = \text{Liquid Limit (LL)} - \text{Plastic Limit (PL)} = 48 - 22 = 26%\). A PI of 26 indicates a highly plastic, cohesive clay that is prone to significant volumetric expansion and shrinkage during moisture cycles. Candidates often add the numbers (\(48 + 22 = 70\)) rather than subtracting them.

### Question 22

Referring to the standard Proctor compaction curve, what occurs if an earthwork contractor compacts structural subgrade soil at a moisture content significantly dry of the Optimum Moisture Content (OMC)?

- A. The soil reaches maximum dry density with minimal compactive effort.
- B. Water fills all void spaces, causing the soil to liquefy under the roller.
- C. High inter-particle friction prevents effective particle rearrangement, leaving large air voids and yielding low dry density.
- D. The soil permanently loses all shear strength due to excess pore water pressure.

**Correct Answer:** C  
**Technical Rationale:** When soil is compacted "dry of optimum," insufficient water is present to lubricate soil grains. Inter-particle friction resists consolidation, leaving high void ratios and low dry density. Adding water lubricates the particles up to the OMC peak; adding water beyond that point displaces soil grains with water, lowering the density along the wet branch of the curve.

### Question 23

When designing a subsurface perimeter foundation drainage system (French drain) to relieve hydrostatic pressure against a basement retaining wall, how must the gravel envelope and geotextile fabric be arranged?

- A. Perforated drain pipe placed invert-down at the footing base, enveloped in washed crushed stone, and completely wrapped in a geotextile filter fabric to prevent fine soil intrusion.
- B. Solid smooth pipe placed at the top of the backfill, wrapped in plastic sheeting, and backfilled with impermeable clay.

**Correct Answer:** A

### Question 24

In cold-weather architectural specifications, what is the primary structural mechanism of air-entraining admixtures (ASTM C260) in concrete, and what is their side effect on compressive strength?

- A. They introduce billions of microscopic, spherical air bubbles that relieve internal hydrostatic pressure during freezing, but reduce compressive strength by roughly 5% per 1% of entrained air.
- B. They chemically neutralize water freezing points down to \(-20^\circ\text{C}\) without altering final compressive strength.
- C. They accelerate cement hydration to generate high internal heat, doubling 28-day tensile capacity.
- D. They react with calcium hydroxide to produce impermeable crystalline structures, increasing compressive strength by 20%.

**Correct Answer:** A  
**Technical Rationale:** Air-entraining admixtures create stable microscopic air voids (\(0.05\text{ mm}\) diameter) that provide expansion chambers for freezing water, preventing spalling. However, each 1% of entrained air typically reduces concrete compressive strength by 2% to 6%. Candidates often miss this strength penalty, assuming admixtures improve all mechanical properties simultaneously.

### Question 25

Why is the indiscriminate use of calcium chloride (\(\text{CaCl}\_2\)) as an accelerating admixture strictly prohibited or heavily limited in structural reinforced concrete by ACI 318?

- A. It causes flash setting within 60 seconds of batching.
- B. It introduces chloride ions that depassivate the protective oxide film on embedded steel rebar, inducing rapid electrochemical pitting and corrosion.
- C. It reacts with silica aggregates to trigger immediate catastrophic expansion.
- D. It prevents the concrete from ever reaching its specified 28-day design strength.

**Correct Answer:** B  
**Technical Rationale:** Calcium chloride accelerates initial set and early strength gain, but it introduces free chloride ions (\(\text{Cl}^-\)) that break down the passive alkaline layer protecting embedded steel reinforcing bars. This accelerates corrosion, spalling the concrete cover. High-performance projects prohibit \(\text{CaCl}\_2\), opting instead for non-chloride accelerators like calcium nitrite.

### Question 26

During an on-site ASTM C143 slump test, the freshly consolidated concrete specimen shears off along an inclined plane, causing half the cone to slide downward asymmetrically. How must the testing technician interpret and proceed with this result?

- A. Record the average subsidence height between the high and low sides as the official slump value.
- B. The test indicates a "shear slump," reflecting a lack of plastic cohesion; the test is invalid and must be immediately repeated with a fresh sample from the same batch.
- C. The concrete is accepted as self-consolidating concrete (SCC).
- D. The batch is instantly rejected and ordered removed from the project site without retesting.

**Correct Answer:** B  
**Technical Rationale:** ASTM C143 states that if a shear slump occurs (where a portion of the specimen shears off along an inclined plane), the test is invalid because it indicates poor mix cohesion rather than uniform workability. The technician must discard the specimen and retest another portion of the same batch. If shear slump occurs twice in a row, the mix lacks the cohesion necessary for placement.

### Question 27

Under standard building construction specifications and fair weather conditions (\(>10^\circ\text{C}\)), what is the minimum recommended curing time before stripping vertical formwork from structural columns and loadbearing walls, compared to horizontal suspended slab soffits?

- A. Columns/walls: 24 to 48 hours; Suspended slab soffits: 14 to 21 days (with reshoring as engineered).
- B. Columns/walls: 14 days; Suspended slab soffits: 24 hours.
- C. Both columns and suspended slabs must retain forms for exactly 28 days.
- D. Columns/walls: 7 days; Suspended slab soffits: 7 days.

**Correct Answer:** A  
**Technical Rationale:** Vertical forms (columns and walls) carry no gravity flexural loads; they only retain the lateral pressure of the freshly placed concrete and can be stripped after 24 to 48 hours once the surface is sufficiently hard. Horizontal floor soffits carry both self-weight and construction live loads, requiring 14 to 21 days along with reshoring until the concrete develops sufficient flexural and shear capacity.

### Question 28

What chemical reaction produces white crystalline efflorescence on the exterior surface of newly constructed masonry and concrete walls?

- A. Oxidation of ferrous reinforcing bars reacting with atmospheric sulfur.

### Question 29

When specifying mortar for an exterior loadbearing cavity wall under ASTM C270, what structural and material trade-off occurs when choosing Type S mortar instead of Type N mortar?

- A. Type S mortar has higher compressive and flexural bond strength, but lower workability and water retentivity than Type N.
- B. Type S mortar contains more hydrated lime, making it softer and more flexible than Type N.

- B. Polycarbonate possesses exceptional impact toughness and can be cold-formed on-site, but scratches easily and has lower UV and chemical resistance; acrylic offers superior optical clarity and natural UV weatherability, but is more brittle and prone to shattering under impact.
- C. Polycarbonate is a thermosetting resin that cannot be recycled, whereas acrylic is an elastomer.
- D. Both plastics share identical thermal expansion rates and can be glued with standard Portland cement mortar.

**Correct Answer:** B  
**Technical Rationale:** Polycarbonate provides high impact resistance (often classified as bullet- or hurricane-resistant) and fire-retardant self-extinguishing properties, but it requires protective cap layers to resist UV yellowing and is prone to surface scratching. Acrylic offers high light transmittance (\(92%\)) and superior inherent UV resistance without yellowing, but it is brittle and shatters under high localized impact.

### Question 60

An investigation of a modern institutional building reveals peeling interior paint, rusted light-gauge drywall studs, and mold growth along the inside face of the upper-floor exterior envelope. The wall section consists of: exterior stone panels, an unvented air gap, foil-faced insulation board, light-gauge steel studs with batt insulation, and an interior polyethylene sheet placed directly behind the gypsum board. The project is located in a hot, humid tropical climate. What was the fatal building physics detailing error?

- A. Specifying stone veneer instead of an exterior insulated finish system (EIFS).
- B. Installing an interior vapor barrier (polyethylene sheet) in a cooling-dominated hot and humid climate, which created a condensation trap for moisture-laden outdoor air as it cooled against the air-conditioned interior wall face.
- C. Omitting fiberglass batt insulation from within the steel stud cavities.
- D. Using stainless steel shelf angles to support the stone cladding.

**Correct Answer:** B  
**Technical Rationale:** In hot, humid climates, the vapor drive is directed from the warm, humid outdoors toward the cool, air-conditioned indoors. Placing an impermeable vapor retarder (polyethylene) on the indoor side traps migrating moisture against the chilled interior wallboard. The moisture drops below its dew point, liquid water condenses inside the stud cavity, and the light-gauge steel frame rusts alongside mold growth. In cooling-dominated climates, the vapor retarder must be positioned on the exterior side of the thermal envelope.
