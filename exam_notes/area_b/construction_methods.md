### PART 1: EASY QUESTIONS (Questions 1 to 20)

_Core architectural terminology, standard material properties, basic building code thresholds, standard unit conversions, and fundamental historical/theoretical classifications._

#### 1. In surveying and architectural site layout, what is the temporary timber framework erected outside the excavation perimeter used to preserve permanent building lines and elevation datums?

A. Sheet piling  
B. Batter boards  
C. Soldier beams  
D. Ledger strips

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** Batter boards consist of horizontal timber boards nailed to vertical posts stationed outside the excavation line to establish reference planes for building corners, footing trench depths, and wall alignments. A common examinee error is confusing batter boards with ledger strips, which are horizontal members fastened to beams or walls solely to support floor joist seats.

#### 2. What nominal lumber dimensions are standardly required for concrete formwork serving columns, beam bottoms, and girder soffits?

A. 1-inch nominal stock  
B. 2-inch nominal stock  
C. 3-inch nominal stock  
D. 4-inch nominal stock

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** Heavy structural formwork components directly resisting severe flexural gravity head pressures—such as column shells, beam bottoms, and girder soffits—mandate 2-inch nominal lumber. Candidates frequently confuse this with 1-inch nominal stock, which is permitted only for beam sides, girder sides, and slab sheathing panels.

#### 3. What is the minimum thickness required for exterior waterproof Grade "A" veneer plywood when utilized as concrete form sheathing?

A. 1/4 inch (6.4 mm)  
B. 3/8 inch (9.5 mm)  
C. 1/2 inch (12.7 mm)  
D. 3/4 inch (19.0 mm)

- **Correct Answer:** C
- **Technical Rationale:** Building construction standards mandate a minimum thickness of 1/2 inch (12.7 mm) for exterior Grade "A" veneer plywood forms to prevent bulging under hydrostatic concrete pressure. The common board trap is selecting 3/4 inch, which is common for heavy architectural casework and subfloors but exceeds the baseline code minimum for formwork.

#### 4. Under the Unified Soil Classification System (USCS), which soil group exhibits the highest presumptive bearing capacity of 10,000 psf (479 kPa)?

A. SW (Well-graded sand)  
B. CL (Inorganic clay)  
C. GW (Well-graded gravel)  
D. ML (Inorganic silt)

- **Correct Answer:** C
- **Technical Rationale:** Clean, well-graded gravels (GW) and poorly graded gravels (GP) possess the highest presumptive bearing capacity at 10,000 psf (479 kPa) due to dense mechanical particle interlock. Examinees often confuse GW with clean well-graded sands (SW), which are limited to a presumptive bearing capacity of 7,500 psf (359 kPa).

#### 5. What is the statutory minimum depth below finished grade or regional frost line for all permanent building foundation footings?

A. 12 inches (305 mm)  
B. 18 inches (457 mm)  
C. 24 inches (610 mm)  
D. 36 inches (914 mm)

- **Correct Answer:** C
- **Technical Rationale:** Standard building regulations require footings to bear at a minimum depth of 24 inches (610 mm) below undisturbed finished ground level to prevent frost heave and surface erosion. Candidates frequently fall for 12 inches or 18 inches, which are common depths for non-loadbearing boundary garden walls rather than permanent structural footings.

#### 6. What is the minimum required concrete protective cover for main reinforcing steel cast directly against and permanently exposed to earth?

A. 1-1/2 inches (38 mm)  
B. 2 inches (50 mm)  
C. 3 inches (75 mm)  
D. 4 inches (100 mm)

- **Correct Answer:** C
- **Technical Rationale:** Cast-in-place concrete permanently in contact with earth requires a minimum protective cover of 3 inches (75 mm) to prevent groundwater corrosion of the rebar. A frequent board trap is selecting 2 inches or 1-1/2 inches, which apply only to concrete formed against formwork and later exposed to weather.

#### 7. What is the absolute minimum center-to-center driving spacing permitted for structural wood foundation piles?

A. 1'-6" (450 mm)  
B. 2'-0" (600 mm)  
C. 2'-6" (750 mm)  
D. 3'-0" (900 mm)

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** Wood foundation piles must never be spaced closer than 2'-0" (600 mm) on centers to prevent severe subterranean lateral displacement and pile heaving during driving. While standard engineering practice favors 2'-6" to 3'-0" on center, 2'-0" represents the absolute minimum code boundary.

#### 8. In traditional timber balloon framing, what horizontal member is housed or notched into exterior studs to support second-story floor joists?

A. Sole plate  
B. Ribbon (ribband)  
C. Drop girt  
D. Raised girt

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** A ribbon (or ribband) is a 1x4 or 1x6 inch continuous horizontal board notched into the interior faces of full-height balloon studs to seat upper-level joists. A common trap is selecting a girt, which is a heavy horizontal timber framed directly between major posts in combination or braced timber frames.

D. 60% of actual stud depth

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** Bored holes in loadbearing studs cannot exceed 40% of the stud depth (expandable to 60% only if the stud is doubled). Examinees frequently confuse this with the 25% limitation, which applies strictly to edge notching of loadbearing members.

#### 10. What is the minimum overlap required when wood top plates are doubled at wall corners and partition intersections?

The distractor of 24 inches or 36 inches is a classic board trap based on standard stud spacing multiples.

#### 14. What is the primary operational distinction between pre-tensioned and post-tensioned prestressed concrete systems?

A. Pre-tensioning uses cables; post-tensioning uses rigid steel rods  
B. Pre-tensioning stresses tendons before concrete placement; post-tensioning stresses tendons after the concrete hardens  
C. Pre-tensioning is exclusively cast on the jobsite; post-tensioning is performed only in precast factories  
D. Pre-tensioning relies on mechanical end anchorages; post-tensioning relies solely on tendon surface chemical bonding

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** Pre-tensioning tensions high-strength steel strands against external abutments prior to pouring concrete, transferring precompression via bond after curing; post-tensioning tensions tendons through cast-in-place conduits after concrete has hardened. #### 15. What geometric feature distinguishes an American Standard Beam (S-shape) from a modern Wide-Flange (W-shape) structural steel beam?

A. S-shapes possess parallel flange faces; W-shapes have a 1:6 internal flange slope

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** Hot-rolled American Standard (S) beams feature a distinct 1:6 internal flange slope originating from historic rolling mill profiles, whereas Wide-Flange (W) shapes feature parallel inner and outer flange surfaces. Option A represents the inverted classic board trap.

#### 16. In the structural analysis of planar pin-connected trusses, what condition indicates that the framework is an ideal, statically determinate structure?

A. \(m = 2j - 3\)  
B. \(m > 2j - 3\)  
C. \(m < 2j - 3\)  
D. \(m = 3j - 2\)

- **Correct Answer:** A
- **Technical Rationale:** The static determinacy equation for planar trusses is \(m = 2j - 3\), where \(m\) equals the number of members and \(j\) equals the number of pin joints. If \(m > 2j - 3\), the truss is statically indeterminate (redundant); if \(m < 2j - 3\), it is structurally unstable.

A. 3-Hour rating; no glass permitted

C. 3/4 Hour rating; 1,296 sq. in. (0.84 sq. m.) per leaf

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** UL Class B fire doors provide a 1-1/2 hour fire rating for vertical shafts and stair enclosures, permitting a maximum of 100 sq. in. of 1/4" wired glass per leaf. Option A defines a Class A fire wall door (3-hour, zero glass), while Option C defines a Class C corridor partition door.

#### 18. What window type provides 100% natural unobstructed ventilating area relative to its rough wall opening?

A. Double-hung window  
B. Horizontal sliding window  
C. Casement window  
D. Fixed sash window

- **Correct Answer:** C
- **Technical Rationale:** Casement windows swing entirely clear of the frame on projecting hinges, yielding a 100% net ventilating aperture. Double-hung and sliding windows are limited to a maximum theoretical ventilation area of 50% because the sashes overlap within the frame.

A. 1 part Lentrek TC to 10 parts water  
B. 1 part Lentrek TC to 25 parts water  
C. 1 part Lentrek TC to 50 parts water

#### 21. An architect is verifying the squaring of a rectangular foundation layout measuring 9.00 meters wide by 12.00 meters long using a steel tape. What exact diagonal cross-measurement must be confirmed between opposite corners?

A. 14.50 meters  
B. 15.00 meters  
C. 15.75 meters  
D. 16.00 meters

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** Applying the Euclidean 3-4-5 right triangle proportion where width is \(3 \times 3.0\text{ m} = 9.0\text{ m}\) and length is \(4 \times 3.0\text{ m} = 12.0\text{ m}\), the diagonal equals \(\sqrt{9^2 + 12^2} = \sqrt{81 + 144} = \sqrt{225} = 15.00\text{ meters}\). Examinees making hasty approximations without computing the square root of the sum often guess 14.50 or 15.75 meters.

C. \(0.50H\) to \(0.60H\)

- **Correct Answer:** C

The \(0.80H\) to \(0.90H\) ratio (Option D) is reserved strictly for surcharge conditions where the retained earth slopes steeply upward.

#### 23. ⚠️ [VISUAL REFERENCE REQUIRED: Isolated spread footing plan and section, highlighting the critical section for one-way beam shear at distance d from column face, and two-way punching shear perimeter at d/2]

In an isolated reinforced concrete spread footing with an effective depth of \(d = 500\text{ mm}\), at what respective geometric locations must the critical sections for one-way (beam) shear and two-way (punching) shear be evaluated? #### 28. ⚠️ [VISUAL REFERENCE REQUIRED: Mechanical detail of a scarfed bending splice, showing the square cut upper half in compression, the beveled lower half, and the bolted tension fishplate at the bottom surface]

What structural mechanics justify this detailing? A. The top square cut resists tensile forces, while the bottom bevel resists shear

#### 34. In continuous reinforced concrete beam detailing, within which zone of the clear span is lap splicing of the top (negative moment) longitudinal reinforcing bars strictly prohibited?

A. In the middle third (\(L/3\)) of the span  
B. Within a distance of twice the beam depth (\(2d\)) from the midspan  
C. Within a distance of \(L/3\) from the face of the column support

- **Correct Answer:** C

#### 40. What physical mechanism differentiates bentonite clay waterproofing from standard applied liquid bituminous membrane systems?

A. Bentonite creates a chemical covalent bond with concrete calcium silicate hydrates  
B. Bentonite consists of dry clay panels that swell into an impervious gelatinous barrier when hydrated under confinement

#### 41. During deep basement excavation adjacent to a 5-story historic masonry building, the contractor proposes unbraced open-cut excavation without underpinning. Legally and technically, what primary liability and geotechnical failure mechanism must the project architect flag?

A. The contractor is exempt from underpinning if the excavation is within property setbacks; soil will fail solely by liquefaction  
B. The constructor is legally liable for lateral and vertical support of adjacent structures; removal of lateral confinement causes soil shear failure and catastrophic differential settlement of the existing footings  
C. Adjacent footings will fail only if the excavation encounters the permanent water table; lateral soil pressure is negligible above it  
D. Building codes require underpinning only when the excavation depth exceeds 30 meters

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** Building regulations mandate that anyone excavating below adjacent footings is legally and financially responsible for providing shoring and underpinning. Removing lateral soil confinement reduces the shearing strength of the foundation bed, triggering bearing capacity failure and differential settlement in the adjoining structure.

#### 42. ⚠️ [VISUAL REFERENCE REQUIRED: Strap (cantilever) footing detail, showing exterior property line column, isolated exterior footing, continuous rigid concrete strap beam, and interior isolated footing with moment diagram]

C. To allow the exterior footing to bear on soft topsoil while the interior footing bears on bedrock

#### 45. A timber column with an effective buckling length (\(L_e\)) of 3.6 meters has a cross-section of \(100\text{ mm} \times 100\text{ mm}\). What is its slenderness ratio (\(L_e/d\)), and does it comply with the maximum building code limit of 50?

- **Technical Rationale:** The slenderness ratio is calculated as \(L_e / d = 3,600\text{ mm} / 100\text{ mm} = 36\). Because this value is less than the statutory maximum limit of 50, the column is structurally compliant against premature lateral elastic buckling under axial load.

#### 46. An architect inspecting a Concrete Hollow Block (CHB) wall footing notes that the longitudinal rebar ratio is only 0.0010 (\(0.1%\)) of the concrete footing cross-sectional area. What structural pathology is this footing vulnerable to, and what is the required code threshold?

A. Shear failure; required steel ratio is 0.050 to 0.080  
B. Transverse shrinkage and differential settlement cracking; required steel ratio is 0.002 to 0.003 (\(0.2%\) to \(0.3%\)) of the footing area  
C. Concrete crushing; required steel ratio is 0.010 to 0.080  
D. Excessive soil heave; footings require zero longitudinal reinforcement

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** Masonry wall footings require a minimum longitudinal steel ratio of \(0.002\) to \(0.003\) (\(0.2%\) to \(0.3%\) of the gross footing area) to resist longitudinal bending, thermal shrinkage, and differential foundation settlement. A ratio of 0.0010 provides insufficient tensile capacity, risking transverse cracking.

#### 48. A rectangular reinforced concrete tied column measures \(400\text{ mm} \times 400\text{ mm}\) and is reinforced with four 25 mm diameter longitudinal bars and 10 mm diameter lateral ties. What is the maximum code-permissible center-to-center spacing for the ties?

A. 250 mm  
B. 400 mm  
C. 480 mm  
D. 500 mm

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** Tie spacing cannot exceed: (1) \(16 \times \text{longitudinal bar diameter} = 16 \times 25 = 400\text{ mm}\); (2) \(48 \times \text{tie diameter} = 48 \times 10 = 480\text{ mm}\); or (3) the least lateral column dimension = \(400\text{ mm}\). The least of these values is 400 mm. Selecting 480 mm ignores the governing constraint of column dimension and bar diameter.

#### 49. In designing a reinforced concrete spiral column, the gross area \(A*g\) is \(1,960\text{ cm}^2\) and the core area \(A*{ch}\) (out-to-out of spiral) is \(1,250\text{ cm}^2\). What governs the volumetric spiral ratio (\(\rho_s\)), and why does the code require such confinement?

A. Confinement transforms brittle concrete core failure into a ductile failure mode by maintaining triaxial lateral compressive stress under high axial loads  
B. Confinement prevents the concrete from expanding thermally in hot climates  
C. Confinement eliminates the need for longitudinal vertical reinforcement  
D. Confinement is required only to anchor the beam bottom rebars

- **Correct Answer:** A
- **Technical Rationale:** Continuous spiral confinement places the concrete core into a state of triaxial compression, preventing sudden spalling and brittle shear failure under axial overload and significantly increasing ductility. The volumetric ratio formula (\(\rho*s \ge 0.45 [A_g/A*{ch} - 1] f'\_c/f_y\)) is engineered to compensate for the loss of the concrete shell if it spalls.

#### 50. In a reinforced concrete continuous floor beam spanning 9.0 meters between columns, an ironworker splices the bottom tension reinforcing bars at exactly 4.5 meters from the column face. Why must the project architect reject this installation?

A. Bottom bars can only be spliced where flexural tension is zero, which occurs exclusively at midspan  
B. The middle third of the span is the zone of maximum positive bending moment; lap splices in tension steel are prohibited here and must be located near supports  
C. Splicing at midspan is prohibited because top bars must be spliced at the exact same location  
D. Building codes prohibit lap splicing of deformed bars in all cast-in-place beams

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** Under gravity loading, maximum positive bending moment (maximum tensile stress in bottom steel) occurs at midspan (\(L/2\)). Splicing bars at midspan introduces slip risk at peak stress. Building codes mandate that bottom positive bars be spliced near the supports (where moments approach zero), while top negative bars must be spliced near midspan.
