## PART 1: EASY QUESTIONS (Questions 1 to 20)

### 1. Which of the following defines the fundamental nature and scope of the History of Architecture as a discipline?

A. A chronological index cataloging political dynasties and their military defenses  
B. A record of man's effort to build beautifully, tracing the origin, growth, and decline of architectural styles across lands and ages  
C. The scientific calculation of allowable stresses and load factors in structural masonry  
D. An empirical catalog of contemporary zoning ordinances and building code setbacks

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** The foundational text defines the History of Architecture as "a record of man's effort to build beautifully," tracing the origin, evolution, and decline of styles across different civilizations and eras. Candidates frequently misidentify history as a mere list of dates or political events, overlooking its core architectural definition as the evolution of aesthetic and structural problem-solving.

---

### 2. Etymologically, the term "megalith" is derived from which combination of classical Greek roots?

A. _Mega_ (great) and _Lithos_ (stone)  
B. _Micros_ (small) and _Lithos_ (stone)  
C. _Mekhane_ (machine) and _Lithos_ (quarry)  
D. _Mesos_ (middle) and _Lithos_ (rock)

- **Correct Answer:** A
- **Technical Rationale:** The word megalith originates directly from the Greek words _mega_ meaning "great" and _lithos_ meaning "stone". A common board-exam pitfall is confusing megalithic terminology with the Mesolithic cultural period or misattributing its roots to Latin rather than Greek.

---

### 3. In prehistoric architecture, what term designates an isolated, upright prehistoric monolith standing alone or arranged in parallel rows?

A. Dolmen  
B. Menhir  
C. Cromlech  
D. Tumulus

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** A menhir is a prehistoric monument consisting of an individual upright monolith, which may stand alone or in aligned linear arrays. Examinees commonly confuse menhirs with dolmens, which require horizontal capstones resting on multiple uprights.

---

### 4. Which of the following prehistoric structures is derived from the Celtic words _daul_ (table) and _maen_ (stone), functioning primarily as a tomb?

A. Menhir  
B. Cromlech  
C. Dolmen  
D. Mastaba

- **Correct Answer:** C
- **Technical Rationale:** The term dolmen originates from _daul_ meaning a table and _maen_ meaning a stone, characterizing a chamber tomb where vertical support stones support a large horizontal slab. Board examinees often confuse Celtic megalithic nomenclature with Egyptian mud-brick mastabas due to their shared funerary function.

---

### 5. What architectural term defines a prehistoric megalithic enclosure composed of large vertical standing stones arranged in a circular formation?

A. Menhir  
B. Dolmen  
C. Cromlech  
D. Pylon

- **Correct Answer:** C
- **Technical Rationale:** A cromlech is defined as a megalithic construction consisting of vertical stones planted in a circular pattern, serving ritual or astronomical functions. Candidates often confuse cromlechs with dolmens, which are table-like chamber tombs rather than circular enclosures.

---

### 6. An artificial earthen mound or barrow constructed over an underground timber or stone tomb corridor is known as a:

A. Tumulus  
B. Pylon  
C. Stela  
D. Ziggurat

- **Correct Answer:** A
- **Technical Rationale:** A tumulus (or passage grave/barrow) consists of an artificial mound of earth and stones raised over an underground burial chamber accessed via an interior corridor. Examinees often confuse a tumulus with a stela, which is an upright carved memorial slab rather than an earthen mound.

- **Correct Answer:** B

### 8. In classical temple layout, what term designates the open vestibule or portico positioned immediately in front of the cella entrance?

A. Posticum  
B. Opisthodomus  
C. Pronaos  
D. Anticum

- **Correct Answer:** C

### 9. Which classical Greek order is characterized by having no dedicated base, resting directly upon the stylobate of the crepidoma?

A. Ionic  
B. Corinthian  
C. Doric  
D. Composite

- **Correct Answer:** C
- **Technical Rationale:** The Greek Doric column has no molded base and rests directly upon the stylobate. A primary board trap is confusing Greek Doric with Roman Doric or Ionic orders, which incorporate molded Attic bases to distribute loads.

### 10. The capital of a classical Greek Doric column is divided into which two primary architectural components?

A. Volute and Caulis  
B. Square abacus and circular bulbous echinus  
C. Dentil and Fascia  
D. Astragal and Plinth

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** The Greek Doric capital consists of two essential members: the flat square abacus at the top and the circular, cushion-like echinus underneath. Volutes belong exclusively to the Ionic and Composite capitals.

### 11. ⚠️ [VISUAL REFERENCE REQUIRED: Profile of a classical moulding showing a convex profile forming approximately a quadrant of a circle]

Which classical moulding profile features a prominent convex curve that projects outward to catch light and cast bold shadows? A. Cavetto  
B. Scotia  
C. Ovolo  
D. Fillet

- **Correct Answer:** C
- **Technical Rationale:** The ovolo is a classical convex moulding whose profile forms a quadrant of a circle or ellipse. Candidates commonly confuse the convex ovolo with the concave cavetto or the deeply recessed scotia.

### 12. ⚠️ [VISUAL REFERENCE REQUIRED: S-shaped classical moulding profile with the upper portion concave and the lower portion convex]

What classical moulding profile is distinguished by a continuous double curve (cyma) whose upper surface is concave and lower surface is convex? A. Cyma Reversa (Ogee)  
B. Cyma Recta  
C. Cavetto  
D. Torus

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** The cyma recta features an S-curve that is concave in its upper portion and convex in its lower portion. Its direct opposite, the cyma reversa (or ogee), starts with an upper convex curve transitioning into a lower concave curve.

### 13. In ancient Egyptian sacred architecture, what monumental gateway guarded temple precincts with twin battered, truncated pyramidal towers?

A. Propylaeum

- **Technical Rationale:** The pylon is the monumental gateway of an Egyptian temple consisting of a pair of truncated pyramidal towers flanking a central portal. The distractors represent gateways of Greek (Propylaeum), Indian (Torana), and Japanese (Torii) traditions.

A. Tepidarium  
B. Calidarium

D. Sudatorium

- **Correct Answer:** C
- **Technical Rationale:** The frigidarium was the designated hall housing the cold-water pool within a Roman bath. A common error is confusing the frigidarium with the warm transitional room (tepidarium) or the sweat room (laconicum/sudatorium).

### 17. Multi-story Roman urban tenement buildings that housed the plebeian population and served as prototypes for modern apartments were called:

A. Domus  
B. Insulae  
C. Villa  
D. Megaron

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** Insulae were 3- to 4-story tenement apartment blocks that housed urban Roman citizens, serving as historic prototypes for modern multi-family condominiums. The domus, by contrast, was a private single-family townhouse.

### 18. In an Early Christian basilican church, what is the colonnaded open forecourt positioned immediately before the narthex?

- **Technical Rationale:** The atrium of an Early Christian basilica is an open forecourt surrounded by colonnaded porticoes containing a central cantharus for ritual ablution. The bema is the transverse space before the apse, while the baldachin is the canopy erected over the altar.

A. Senenmut

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** Imhotep is documented as the earliest architect in recorded history, responsible for engineering Pharaoh Djoser’s monumental stone complex and Step Pyramid at Saqqara. Senenmut lived much later during the New Kingdom.

## PART 2: AVERAGE QUESTIONS (Questions 21 to 40)

### 21. What is the standard classical proportion for the total height of a Greek Doric column (including its capital) relative to its base diameter?

A. 4 to 6 times the base diameter  
B. 7 to 8 times the base diameter  
C. 9 times the base diameter  
D. 10 times the base diameter

- **Correct Answer:** A
- **Technical Rationale:** Greek Doric columns are characterized by sturdy proportions ranging from 4 to 6 times the diameter at the base. Slender proportions such as 9 diameters belong to the Ionic order, while 10 diameters define the Corinthian order.

---

### 22. In classical Greek Doric entablature detailing, what is the standardized proportion of the total entablature height relative to the column's lower diameter?

A. 1 and 1/6 times the diameter  
B. 1 and 3/4 times the lower diameter  
C. 2 and 1/4 times the diameter  
D. 3 times the diameter

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** The Greek Doric entablature height is standardized at 1 and 3/4 times the column's lower diameter. A ratio of 2 and 1/4 times the diameter defines the classical Ionic entablature.

---

### 23. What are the standardized column proportions and flute configurations of the classical Greek Ionic order?

A. Height equal to 6 times the base diameter with 20 flutes separated by sharp arrises  
B. Height equal to 9 times the base diameter with 24 flutes separated by flat fillets  
C. Height equal to 7 times the base diameter with an unfluted shaft  
D. Height equal to 10 times the base diameter with acanthus carvings

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** The Greek Ionic column is slenderer than the Doric, possessing a height equal to 9 times its base diameter and 24 semi-circular flutes separated by flat fillets. The Doric column features 20 shallow flutes separated by sharp arrises.

---

### 24. How does the entablature of the original classical Ionic order in Asia Minor differ proportionally and compositionally from the Greek Doric entablature?

A. It is 1 and 3/4 column diameters in height and includes triglyphs and metopes  
B. It is 2 and 1/4 column diameters in height and originally omitted the frieze band  
C. It is 1/13 the total facade height and features a massive projecting cornice  
D. It possesses an uninterrupted frieze measuring three times the architrave depth

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** The classical Ionic entablature measures 2 and 1/4 times the column diameter and consists of an architrave with fasciae and a cornice, originally omitting a continuous decorative frieze in early Asia Minor examples. Doric entablatures always feature alternating triglyphs and metopes.

---

### 25. According to classical architectural history, who invented the Corinthian capital, and what is its standard height metric relative to the column's diameter?

A. Callimachus; height is 1 and 1/6 column diameters  
B. Ictinus; height is 2 column diameters  
C. Imhotep; height is 1/2 column diameter  
D. Vitruvius; height is 1 and 3/4 column diameters

- **Correct Answer:** A
- **Technical Rationale:** The Corinthian capital was invented by Callimachus (inspired by an acanthus-entwined basket) and is substantially deeper than the Ionic, measuring 1 and 1/6 column diameters high. Examinees often mistake Callimachus for Ictinus, one of the master architects of the Parthenon.

---

### 26. Which classical order was introduced by the Etruscans and adopted by the Romans as a simplified Doric variant with an unfluted shaft and a height of 7 diameters?

A. Composite Order  
B. Tuscan Order  
C. Corinthian Order  
D. Ionic Order

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** The Tuscan order is an Etruscan simplification of the Doric order featuring an unfluted shaft, a molded base, and a height of approximately 7 diameters. The Composite order, developed around 100 AD, merges Ionic volutes with Corinthian acanthus leaves.

### 27. What are the key architectural differentiators between an ancient Egyptian Pyramid and a Mesopotamian Ziggurat regarding geometry, materials, and cardinal alignment?

A. Pyramids have diminishing stepped terraces of mud-brick with corners facing cardinal points; Ziggurats have sloping stone faces aligned to cardinal faces  
B. Pyramids feature smooth sloping stone faces with planar sides facing the cardinal points; Ziggurats have stepped diminishing terraces of mud-brick with corners oriented toward cardinal points  
C. Both structures feature stepped terraces made of granite with sides aligned precisely to the cardinal points  
D. Pyramids functioned as civic meeting places, whereas Ziggurats were exclusively royal chamber tombs

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** Egyptian pyramids feature sloping faces of stone whose planar sides face the cardinal points, whereas Mesopotamian ziggurats feature diminishing stepped terraces of mud-brick whose corners face the cardinal points. Confusing cardinal planar faces with cardinal corner orientations is a classic licensure review trap.

### 29. What type of Roman vault consists of a continuous semicircular masonry arch resting on two parallel bearing walls throughout its entire length?

A. Cross Vault  
B. Hemispherical Cupola  
C. Wagon / Barrel / Tunnel Vault  
D. Pendentive Dome

- **Correct Answer:** C
- **Technical Rationale:** A wagon, barrel, or tunnel vault is a semicircular arch extrusion carried continuously across two parallel load-bearing walls. Cross (groin) vaults are formed by the perpendicular intersection of two barrel vaults.

### 30. A Roman cross vault (groin vault) is formed geometrically by:

A. Extruding a pointed arch along an inclined axis  
B. The intersection at right angles of two semicircular vaults of equal span over a square bay  
C. Revolving a semicircular arch around its central vertical axis  
D. Corbeling horizontal courses of stone masonry over an octagonal chamber

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** A cross vault is generated by the intersection of two semicircular barrel vaults of identical span crossing at right angles, concentrating loads at four corner points over a square bay. Revolving an arch creates a hemispherical dome or cupola.

## PART 3: DIFFICULT QUESTIONS (Questions 41 to 60)

### 41. Architectural Forensic Investigation: Why did the royal builders of Pharaoh Sneferu abruptly alter the angle of slope midway up the Bent Pyramid at Dahshur?

A. To deliberately replicate the sacred profile of the constellation Orion  
B. Because the planned 150-meter-high pyramid was structurally too bold and the unstable ground subsided, forcing designers to decrease the slope to reduce dead load  
C. Because pharaonic funds were depleted, requiring an emergency flat-topped platform  
D. Due to a royal decree commanding an imitation of a French mansard roof profile

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** The Bent Pyramid of Sneferu at Dahshur was initially projected to reach 150 meters with a steep incline, but because the foundation began to settle and yield under the enormous masonry mass, the builders altered the slope angle to reduce dead weight and prevent collapse. Candidates often mistakenly assume the bend was a purely aesthetic choice or solar alignment marker.

### 42. ⚠️ [VISUAL REFERENCE REQUIRED: Transverse cross-section through the Great Pyramid of Khufu at Giza detailing the King's Chamber]

What structural mechanism was engineered directly above the King’s Chamber in the Great Pyramid of Khufu to protect the burial chamber from structural collapse? A. A reinforced barrel vault made of burnt clay bricks bonded with pozzolana mortar  
B. Five tiers of massive granite relieving beams separated by hollow voids, surmounted by a gabled roof of inclined stone slabs  
C. A system of interlocking timber king-post trusses spanning across stone corbels  
D. A monolithic corbeled dome carved directly out of the bedrock plateau

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** Khufu’s King’s Chamber is protected by five tiers of massive granite relieving beams with intermediate stress-relieving cavities, topped by a pitched gabled roof of limestone slabs that diverts millions of tons of superincumbent masonry dead load away from the ceiling. Distractors cite Roman concrete vaults or medieval timber trusses that did not exist in Old Kingdom Egypt.

### 43. In the construction of the Parthenon in Athens, what deliberate optical corrections were executed on the stylobate, entablature, and columns?

A. Perfect horizontal straightness and vertical alignment verified by plumb lines  
B. Convex upward curvature along horizontal lines and inward inclination of vertical axes to prevent visual sagging and outward falling  
C. Concave downward curvature on beams and outward tilting of corner columns  
D. Twisting column flutes along a helical spiral to counteract rotational wind shear

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** Classical Greek architects incorporated optical refinements: horizontal lines (stylobate, architrave, entablature) were constructed with a slight convex upward camber to prevent the visual illusion of sagging, while vertical columns were inclined slightly inward to prevent them from appearing to fall outward. Assuming classical architecture strictly employed uncorrected geometric straight lines is a common board-exam misconception.

### 44. What optical deficiency in classical columnar shafts does the application of entasis correct?

A. The illusion of lateral bending caused by earthquakes  
B. The optical illusion of central concavity or waist thinning, corrected by giving the column shaft a slight outward convex curvature  
C. The distortion of carved relief inscriptions under sharp sunlight  
D. Uneven thermal expansion of marble drums exposed to Mediterranean sea winds

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** Entasis is the subtle outward convexity applied to the shaft of a classical column to correct the optical illusion of concavity (central thinning) that occurs when parallel vertical lines are viewed from a distance. Distractors introduce structural mechanics terms that played no role in classical optical theory.

### 45. What chemical and material property of Roman concrete (_opus caementicium_) enabled the construction of colossal unreinforced vaults and domes?

A. Pure lime mixed with river sand, curing solely through surface air drying  
B. Volcanic ash (_pozzolana_) combined with lime, aggregate, and water, enabling hydraulic setting and high structural cohesion  
C. Dehydrated gypsum mixed with shredded papyrus fibers to resist tensile stresses  
D. Portland cement mixed with deformed iron rebar imported from Britannia

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Technical Rationale:** Roman concrete utilized volcanic earth known as _pozzolana_ mixed with lime and stone rubble, creating a hydraulic cement that hardened underwater and formed massive, cohesive monolithic vaults and domes. Portland cement and rebar were 19th-century industrial innovations.

### 46. ⚠️ [VISUAL REFERENCE REQUIRED: Interior perspective of the Pantheon in Rome showing the hemispherical coffered dome and top oculus]

How did Roman master builders reduce the dead load and hoop stresses in the upper reaches of the monolithic dome of the Pantheon? A. By inserting internal timber diagonal trusses throughout the concrete shell

### 48. In High Gothic structural engineering, what is the precise structural load-path function of the pinnacle positioned atop an exterior flying buttress pier?

### 49. Why was the invention and systematic deployment of the pointed arch superior to the semicircular Roman arch in Gothic vaulting?

C. It allowed rectangular bays of unequal spans to be vaulted to uniform crown heights while significantly reducing outward lateral thrust  
D. It prevented rainwater from accumulating on the lead roof sheets above the vault

### 59. Forensic Tectonic Analysis of _Arquitectura Mestiza_: Why did the Spanish colonial _Bahay na Bato_ survive major Philippine earthquakes while unreinforced stone churches suffered catastrophic collapse?
